# PdfCraft roadmap

The milestones, current progress and time estimates to Acrobat Pro feature parity. Keep this up to date:
- **Every session:** update the progress column and add a line to the log.
- **Every milestone:** re-estimate.

Detailed task lists and acceptance tests are in `plan/execution-plan.md` (local-only). This file is the public summary.

**What "parity" means here:** the offline feature set of Acrobat Pro, milestones M0–M14. It excludes Adobe's cloud services (Document Cloud storage, Adobe Sign, the Adobe AI Assistant). Those have no clean-room equivalent; PdfCraft's alternatives are local-first, plus opt-in providers (M13).

## Estimate summary

The unit is **wall-clock hours of agent work** (an AI coding agent working continuously; human review time not included).

**Where we are (2026-10-07, measured by `cargo xtask parity` over 806 tracked Acrobat Pro features; 23 more are Adobe-cloud-only and out of scope):**

| Tier | Features | Shipped | Partial | Shipped % | Weighted % (partial = ½) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0 (must-have for 1.0) | 251 | 222 | 25 | 88.4% | 93.4% |
| P1 | 326 | 174 | 38 | 53.4% | 59.2% |
| P2 | 186 | 14 | 7 | 7.5% | 9.4% |
| P3 | 43 | 1 | 0 | 2.3% | 2.3% |
| **All** | **806** | **411** | **70** | **51.0%** | **55.3%** |

**Effort-weighted parity: ≈ 30–35%.** Feature counts overstate progress: the remaining features include the hardest ones (our own renderer and font engine, editing existing text and reflow, OCR beyond Latin, XFA, PDF/A/X/UA preflight, Office export, long-term signature validation). Weighting each milestone by its estimated size gives about a third of the total work done. See **[Honest assessment](#honest-assessment-2026-10-05)** for what the numbers don't show.

**Observed throughput:** about 72k lines of kept, tested Rust (plus 97 agent tools and ≈ 600 tests) in roughly 65–75 agent-hours since 2026-09-30, about 1.0k lines per agent-hour, with corpus, oracle and visual checks. That is faster than the original plan assumed, so the hour estimates below are revised down from the first plan (2,000–4,000 h).

| Scenario | Remaining hours to full parity (M0–M14) | Calendar time |
|---|---|---|
| One Opus 5.5 agent, continuous | **≈ 600–1,100 h** | ≈ 4–7 weeks |
| 3–5 agents in parallel on separate crates | **≈ 250–450 h wall clock** | ≈ 2–3 weeks |
| With human review, integration and pauses | — | ≈ 2–4 months |

**How the estimate is built:** each milestone's remaining fraction (table below) times its size, re-based on the measured rate. The long poles are M2 (own renderer, ≈ 150–250 h), M7 (editing existing text and images, ≈ 150–250 h), M10 OCR and Office export (≈ 100–150 h), M6 JavaScript engine (≈ 60–100 h), M11 PDF/A/X/UA preflight (≈ 80–140 h) and M12 XFA/compare (≈ 80–140 h). The last 5–10% (odd real-world files, pixel-level polish against Acrobat) costs about as much as a mid-sized milestone.

**What "shipped" means, and doesn't.** A feature is shipped when it exists and at least one specific test covers it. 159 of 398 shipped features rest on exactly one test, and only about 5 cite an external oracle (`pdftotext`, `pdfsig`, OpenSSL, a corpus). Shipped does not mean "as good as Acrobat".

**Robustness, measured.** `main`'s CI was red for 30+ runs until 2026-10-04 (a clippy lint, Windows line endings, flaky perf tests). Once the nightly fuzz job could finish, three 15-minute runs found 7 out-of-memory crashes (one aborted on every platform) and 11 hangs, 7 of them in our own `cos` parser rather than `hayro`. All are fixed or in review, but expect more: keep the nightly fuzz job green and turn every finding into a synthetic test.

## Where we're lacking and where we're going

The gaps that matter most, in priority order. Agents: prefer these over adding P2/P3 features, and check `parity/acrobat-features.toml` notes for the specifics of each.

1. **Our own renderer (M2).** Replace `hayro` with the `model` crate, our font engine and the DisplayList devices (ADR-0004). It turns the 7 rendering P0 partials into shipped features and removes most vendored patches. The largest single lever.
2. **Hardening.** Keep the nightly fuzz job green; fix every crash and hang with a synthetic regression test; finish lazy loading so large files aren't read whole; set performance budgets (`misc.performance-budgets`).
3. **Measure fidelity against Acrobat.** Build a side-by-side harness (render, text, form behaviour) over synthetic fixtures (`plan/acrobat/`, clean-room rules apply). Until then, parity numbers count features, not quality. First step done: `cargo xtask fidelity` measures render and text against Poppler as an external oracle on synthetic fixtures.
4. **Editing existing content (M7).** Fonts, subsets, reflow and CJK. The most visible gap for Pro users.
5. **Pro workflows.** Signatures with timestamps and LTV (M9), OCR beyond Latin (M10), Office import/export (M10), preflight and PDF/A/X/UA (M11), XFA (M12).
6. **1.0 polish (M14).** Code-signed installers, completing UI locale coverage through the existing catalog system, keyboard-only operation, and a full screen-reader audit.

Decided against for now (owner, 2026-10-05): self-installing updates and an update check at start. Help ▸ Check for updates is manual only.

## M7 current work and remaining text parity

The 2026-10-11 wave added exact existing-page font selection and paragraph formatting fidelity.
The 2026-10-12 checkpoint adds a run-preserving editable paragraph model, Unicode-scalar selection
formatting, source character-code and TJ spacing preservation, independent style changes, and
an actual-PDF draft preview. Its acceptance covers selecting a word, preserving surrounding
runs, reflow, no-op/error history, undo/redo and interoperable save/reopen. It keeps the overall
editing feature **partial**, and does not increase the historical overall effort estimate.

Further M7 work includes skewed/reflected paragraphs, complete text-rendering/stroke/
ExtGState preservation (currently refused), robust logical paragraph identity
through complex layouts and content-stream boundaries, source preservation for whole edits of skewed or reflected paragraphs (upright and rotated ones now use the run path), run decorations, tagged-content
preservation, broader encodings, complex shaping, find/replace and lists. The typing surface still
uses interface fonts; the explicit rendered preview shows the actual PDF. Character offsets are
Unicode scalars, and indivisible multi-scalar PDF glyphs are protected against partial selection.

[The 2026-10-12 inventory](docs/parity-inventory-2026-10-12.md) records every area's remaining
implementation count and the evidence required beyond manifest labels.

## Critical path

M0 → M1 → M2 → M3 → M4 must happen in order. After M4, M5–M12 can run in parallel across crates. The long poles are M7 (content editing), M9 (signatures) and M12 (XFA).

## Risks most likely to push estimates up

- Fidelity of text editing: fonts, subsets, reflow.
- XFA dynamic layout.
- Real-world signature chains and revocation checks.
- The rendering long tail: Type3 fonts, broken fonts, shadings.
- Correctness of PDF/A and PDF/UA conversion.
- CPU rendering performance on the web.
- Hostile input: every fuzz run so far has found new crashes or hangs.
- Unmeasured fidelity: without an Acrobat comparison harness, quality gaps surface as user reports.

## Log

- **2026-10-17 (M10, Searchable Image output):** Recognize Text ▸ Output now offers Searchable Image next to Searchable Image (Exact). It turns on deskew, and when recognition finds a page skewed or turned (auto-rotate), the page itself is straightened after the text layer is added: the skew is undone about the visible page centre by a `q … cm` / `Q` wrapper around all of its content (`pdfcraft_edit::transform_page`, streams tagged `Straighten`; later marks stay outside it), and quarter turns go into `/Rotate`, so the invisible words turn with the image they cover; then the recognized pages' images finer than the chosen resolution are downsampled to it, each keeping its own compression (`optimize::downsample_page_images`). One undo step. `ocr_recognize`, `ocr_prepare` and `ocr_recognize_files` take `output: exact | straighten`. Tests: a synthetic skewed scan (6°, on pages with `/Rotate` 0 and 90) measures under 0.5° after straightening, keeps its text layer, and Exact leaves it unchanged; StraightenPage refuses singular or non-finite transforms and undoes; a 288 ppi image is downsampled to 72 ppi in one undoable step and Exact keeps it; the dialog switches output; tool arguments are validated before recognition. `ocr.searchable-image` is shipped.

- **2026-10-12 (M7, rotated paragraph editing):** Paragraphs turned by any angle (Tm, CTM or both, with separate horizontal and vertical scales along the text axes) are now edited through the run-preserving path. Text lines are measured in a local text frame (user space in the unit basis of the text axes, the inverse of their normalized matrix; upright text maps to user space exactly), so line joining, inferred gaps from projected source end points, paragraph grouping, row baselines and widths follow the text's own axes; a single turned line grows towards the margin it runs to, and emitted Tm/cm keep the source axes at full precision. Skewed or reflected text still refuses range edits and keeps the legacy whole-paragraph re-encoding. Verified: edit lib 92 (new: frame unit test; kerning/gaps, rows/reflow, range recolour + underline, rises, margin and skew refusal over 90/180/270/30/-45 degree fixtures with an independent glyph-position interpreter), automation 120 (new end-to-end test: 90/180/30 degree edits keep every pixel off the changed word through undo/redo, save and reopen), engine lib 140 + 4 XFA tests that fail only under the 8 GB address-space limit (thread spawn, pass without it), UI editing 80, clippy (edit, automation) clean; a `pdfcraft-cli run` workflow checked with Poppler kept every untouched word box and every pixel outside the edited word on 90/180/30 degree fixtures (30 degree words are fragmented differently by Poppler; characters and union box equal); `cargo xtask fidelity` passed against its baseline. `edit.text-edit` stays partial; estimates unchanged.

- **2026-10-12 (M11, Optimizer codecs: CCITT G4, LZW to Flate, object-stream option):** `pdfcraft-filters` gains an original CCITT Group 4 (T.6) encoder, checked against the independent hayro-ccitt decoder (every bitmap up to 12 pixels in every shape, awkward widths, long runs, a proptest) and against Poppler (`pdfimages` decodes G4 copies and 17 awkward shapes to the exact source bitmaps, `pdftoppm` renders before/after pixel-identically, `pdfimages -list` reports `ccitt`). The Optimizer recompresses one-bit images and image masks as G4 (or Flate) when smaller and can downsample them (area average, majority with ties to ink: not bicubic); Clean Up re-encodes LZW streams as Flate only when they decode completely, through `Stream::decoded_complete`/`decode_complete`, the same strict rules (end-of-data code, direct filter description, 32/128 MiB bounds) the preflight LZW fix now uses instead of its private bit scanner. The Optimizer has a Monochrome Images row and Clean Up check boxes for LZW and object streams; `doc_optimize` takes `mono`, `lzw_to_flate` and `compress_structure` (translations in all 14 catalogs). Executed: filters+optimize+preflight 99/99, cos lib 44/44, automation 161/161 (incl. 1 new end-to-end test), engine optimize 1/1, ui-egui i18n 74/74 and the optimizer dialog kittest, clippy on the seven touched crates with warnings denied, `xtask parity`. Synthetic fixtures only; no JBIG2, no Acrobat comparison; no estimate increase.

- **2026-10-12 (M7, whole-paragraph source preservation):** Replacing an upright paragraph's whole text now goes through the run-preserving path instead of re-encoding every word from whitespace-collapsed Unicode. Untouched characters keep their source codes, styles and TJ adjustments (written back as the same TJ numbers), typed repeated spaces survive (line breaks and tabs become one space each, CR LF once), and source rows the edit does not reach keep their line breaks; an edit that overflows its row still wraps. ### OCR review and cancellation follow-up (2026-10-12)

Recognition can now remain a document-version-bound draft for word review, correction and explicit apply/cancel. Corrections retain geometry and source pixels, support WinAnsi accents, and commit as one undoable operation. The engine rejects stale revisions, validates image/text/geometry limits and discards all partial results on cancellation. Invisible text state is scoped so later visible text stays visible. The headless workflow is `ocr_prepare` → `ocr_review` / `ocr_correct` → `ocr_apply` or `ocr_cancel`.

This closes part of correction workflow, not multilingual recognition or confidence-based suspects. English remains the only validated recognition language; rotation detection, deskew, existing-layer correction, arbitrary Unicode output, editable-text output and image cleanup remain open. Model-free engine fixtures and model-backed automation/UI cases are listed in `crates/ocr/README.md`; an absent-model skip must not be counted as a recognition pass. Milestone percentage estimates are unchanged.

Recognition now requires both copy and modify permission (the permissions password lifts restrictions), checked before any page is rendered or a model runs, and again whenever a draft is shown, corrected or applied; encrypted synthetic fixtures cover each combination in the engine and automation tools. `PDFCRAFT_MODELS_REQUIRED=1` turns a missing-model skip into a failure, and Linux CI now fetches the pinned models and sets it (not yet observed in a CI run). Executed locally with the pinned models: the review/apply/save/reopen CLI workflow on an original Inter-set scan recognized all 10 words with unchanged page pixels. Running the older engine fixture with the real models for the first time showed the bundled model misreading "quick" as "auick" in a 150 dpi Helvetica scan; that fixture now scans at 300 dpi. These are observations on a few synthetic pages, not accuracy claims.
